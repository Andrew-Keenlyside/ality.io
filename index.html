Integrating new work into complex supply chains

Large projects run on supply chains many tiers deep. Bringing in a new supplier is slow, because each side guards what matters most to it.

Suppliers

You make something a large project could use: a component, a material, a vessel or a service. Proving it fits usually means long questionnaires and sharing detail you would rather keep.

Project teams

Developers, manufacturers and main contractors need new capacity, but every evaluation asks you to open up designs, schedules and terms to an outsider.

Confidential by design

Each side briefs us separately and keeps its own material. We work from a shared network of the supply chain, built from public data and expert input, so the answer is sharp without either side opening up.

Stays with the supplier

  • Product specifications and test data
  • Prices and capacity
  • Proprietary designs and IP
Confidential boundary

Stays with the project team

  • Designs and requirements
  • Sourcing and schedules
  • Commercial terms

Only the result crosses. Each side agrees beforehand what of its material the assessment may draw on.

  1. Each side briefs us separately

    The supplier describes the product. The project team describes what it needs. Neither sees the other's material.

  2. We work out the fit

    Technical, commercial and logistical requirements, checked against the supply chain the product would join.

  3. Both sides get the same answer

    One fit-and-risk assessment: where it fits, where the gaps are, and what would close them.

What we look at

Each assessment is written for one product and one supply chain, and both sides read the same version.

Technical fit
Specifications, standards and certification, checked against what the project requires.
Capacity and timing
Whether volumes and lead times match the build programme.
Logistics
The ports, vessels, transport and storage the product depends on.
Supply chain exposure
Single sources, concentration, and risks further up the chain.
Next steps
What to prove, test or change to make the fit stronger.
